# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Criticise code, not the person who wrote it. Assume the
other person is competent and acting in good faith until you have real evidence
otherwise — and if you do have that evidence, raise it privately rather than in
a thread.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on the work: what is wrong, why it matters, and ideally what
  would be better. "This is broken" helps nobody; "this crashes on a zero-width
  selection because `w` is used as a divisor" helps everyone.
- Accept feedback on your own work the same way. A review that finds a bug has
  done you a favour, even when the tone is blunter than you would like.
- Remember that contributors differ in experience, language, timezone and how
  much time they have. A question that seems obvious to you was not obvious to
  the person asking.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind.
- Demeaning comments about someone's identity — including but not limited to
  age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality,
  appearance, race, religion, or sexual orientation.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberately derailing threads.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, commit messages, and anywhere
else someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour is a problem, email the maintainer rather than escalating
in public. Reports will be handled discreetly. The maintainer may edit, remove
or reject comments, commits, issues and pull requests that do not fit this
document, and may block repeat offenders.

This project is small, so there is no committee — just a person trying to keep
it a reasonable place to work. If that person is the problem, say so plainly;
that is fair game.
